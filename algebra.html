FOUNDATION COURSE

Algebra: Concepts & Connections

Build a durable algebra foundation through functions, equations, modeling, data, and multiple representations. The course is organized around Georgia’s Algebra: Concepts & Connections framework and uses broadly transferable high-school algebra skills.

Georgia A standardsFunctions & modelingPractice + applications
COURSE MAP

Learn the ideas that connect.

Each module is designed to move from concept → representation → practice → application. Use the map as a course outline or jump directly to a topic.

01

Numerical & Algebraic Reasoning

Rational and irrational numbers, radicals, exponents, expressions, structure, and precise symbolic work.

02

Linear Functions & Equations

Rates of change, slope, intercepts, equations, inequalities, systems, and real-world linear models.

03

Quadratic Functions

Factoring, completing the square, quadratic formula, graphs, zeros, transformations, and optimization.

04

Exponential Functions

Growth and decay, percent change, compound growth, transformations, and interpreting parameters.

05

Functions & Representations

Domain and range, notation, composition, inverses, tables, graphs, equations, and contextual meaning.

06

Geometry Through Algebra

Coordinate geometry, distance, midpoint, slope, area, perimeter, and algebraic models of geometric situations.

07

Data & Statistical Reasoning

Univariate and bivariate data, distributions, association, models, residual thinking, and interpreting data in context.

08

Modeling Capstone

Choose a function, state assumptions, fit/compare models, interpret results, and communicate conclusions.

Standards & alignment

Georgia uses A.MP mathematical practices alongside content standards such as A.PAR (Patterning & Algebraic Reasoning), A.FGR (Functional & Graphical Reasoning), A.GSR (Geometric & Spatial Reasoning), and A.DSR (Data & Statistical Reasoning). The site is Georgia-first but the underlying algebra topics are intentionally useful across common U.S. Algebra 1 pathways.

STUDY SMARTER

Don't just get the answer.

For every problem, ask: What is changing? What representation makes it visible? What assumptions am I making? Can I explain why my answer is reasonable?
