Geometry: Concepts & Connections

Develop geometric reasoning through proof, constructions, transformations, congruence, similarity, circles, measurement, trigonometry, probability, and modeling.

COURSE MAP

Learn the ideas that connect.

Each module is designed to move from concept → representation → practice → application. Use the map as a course outline or jump directly to a topic.

01

Geometric Foundations

Points, lines, angles, constructions, notation, coordinate reasoning, and the language of geometry.

02

Reasoning & Proof

Conjectures, deductive reasoning, conditional statements, proofs, counterexamples, and mathematical communication.

03

Transformations & Congruence

Translations, rotations, reflections, dilations, rigid motions, congruence, and transformation-based reasoning.

04

Similarity & Proportional Reasoning

Similarity criteria, scale factors, proportional relationships, indirect measurement, and geometric modeling.

05

Quadrilaterals & Polygons

Properties of quadrilaterals, polygon angle relationships, coordinate methods, and classification.

06

Circles

Arcs, chords, tangents, inscribed angles, equations of circles, and connections between algebra and geometry.

07

Measurement & 3D Geometry

Area, surface area, volume, density-style applications, cross-sections, and spatial visualization.

08

Right-Triangle Trigonometry

Sine, cosine, tangent, special triangles, the unit-circle connection, and solving contextual problems.

09

Probability & Statistics

Conditional probability, compound events, simulations, data interpretation, and geometric probability.

10

Geometry Capstone

Build and defend a model using diagrams, equations, measurements, transformations, and evidence.

Standards & alignment

Georgia’s Geometry: Concepts & Connections course emphasizes geometric, algebraic, graphical, and probabilistic reasoning. Key standards use the G.MP mathematical practices and content strands including GSR (Geometric & Spatial Reasoning), PAR, FGR, and PR (Probabilistic Reasoning). The topic sequence also transfers well to standard U.S. high-school Geometry courses.

STUDY SMARTER

Don't just get the answer.

For every problem, ask: What is changing? What representation makes it visible? What assumptions am I making? Can I explain why my answer is reasonable?
